## 1. What job analysis is and why it matters

Job analysis is the process of systematically collecting and organizing information about a job in order to define its content, duties and working conditions, and the knowledge, skills and abilities a job holder needs. Job analysis studies the job itself, not any particular employee. Its direct outputs are the job description and the job specification, which are often combined in a single document.

Job analysis is the common foundation of several HR activities. Recruitment and selection need it to set clear hiring criteria instead of relying on impressions. Training and development compare people with the job requirements to find skill gaps. Performance management needs clearly defined duties as the basis for appraisal. Compensation design relies on it for information when jobs are evaluated.

## 2. The job analysis process

Job analysis usually has four stages. In the preparation stage, the team sets the purpose, chooses the target jobs and forms a working group. In the investigation stage, it chooses suitable methods and collects information on duties, tasks and working conditions. In the analysis stage, it organizes and verifies the information and summarizes the job elements and requirements. In the completion stage, it writes the job description and specification and revises them regularly as they are used. The purpose shapes the focus: analysis for recruitment pays more attention to job requirements, while analysis for pay pays more attention to the value of the job.

## 3. Common methods

1. Observation: the analyst watches the job holder at work. The information is direct and realistic, but the method does not suit jobs that are mainly mental work.
2. Interview: the analyst talks with job holders and their supervisors. It reveals duties and difficulties in depth, but takes time and can be influenced by the interviewer's own views.
3. Questionnaire: structured questionnaires are sent out. They reach many people and are easy to analyze, but need careful design, and answers may be inaccurate.
4. Work diary: job holders record their activities in time order. The information is detailed and continuous, but it adds to the job holder's workload and things may be left out.

In practice several methods are combined so that they confirm each other.

## 4. Job description and job specification

The written result has two parts. The job description answers "what is this job?": job title and department, duties and main tasks, working conditions and environment, and reporting and working relationships. The job specification answers "who can do it well?": education and field of study, knowledge and skills, relevant work experience, and abilities and personal qualities. Put simply, the job description is about the work, and the job specification is about the person.

Duties change as the business changes, so the document must be reviewed and updated regularly. Otherwise it drifts away from the real work and makes recruitment and appraisal less fair.
